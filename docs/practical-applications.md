# Practical applications

The twelve-week sequence is unchanged. Each week now includes one short `Apply it` section.

| Week | Example | Task |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Rewrite an email | Add the reader, purpose, action, and constraints |
| 2 | Open a morning shift at a drink shop | Ask for information that could change the decision |
| 3 | Define a good article | Compare examples, write with three criteria, and check the draft |
| 4 | Adapt a new-hire process for remote work | Separate what transfers from what changes |
| 5 | Improve a meeting summary | Compare fixed notes with a flawed draft; change one condition at a time |
| 6 | Compare vendors | Mark claims as supported, inferred, or unknown |
| 7 | Keep a brand voice | Separate context, memory, retrieval, and training |
| 8 | Name a product | Generate options, then select against criteria |
| 9 | Write a three-day work report | Check arithmetic and trace each claim to the supplied notes |
| 10 | Group customer feedback | Find repeated classification errors and retest one changed instruction |
| 11 | Find an exception in a return policy | Select the deciding passage and check its citation |
| 12 | Use a learner's task | Define outcomes, steps, checks, limits, and human decisions |

Each section has a short work situation and an action. Weeks 2, 4, 5, and 9–11 now supply fictional source material; Weeks 5 and 9–11 also include a flawed answer or a checkable conclusion. Learners can work through these without a live AI service, then try the same method on their own tasks and save notes locally. The examples are teaching cases, not legal or workplace policy advice.
